The short answer

In a close game, going for it is right far more often than the old habit of punting. Go on 4th & 1 or 2 anywhere on the field, and on 4th & 3 almost anywhere. On our side of the field, punt on 4th & 4 or longer. From about midfield to their 30, go on 4th & 4 to 8; inside their 30, kick the field goal on longer distances. Behind late, go even more: down 3 with 2:00 left, go on 4th & 1 to 6 almost anywhere. Faded squares on the card are close calls where either choice is fine.

The card

Go, punt, or kick?

Rows are the distance to go on 4th down, columns are where the ball is. Each square shows the option that gives us the best chance of winning. Faded squares are close calls, within 1% either way. Hover over any square to see all three options side by side.

The math

How each square is worked out

Each option leads to a few possible next situations. We know how often each one happens, and our chance of winning from each, so we average them. The option with the higher average wins the square.

Go for it

How often we convert × our chance after a first down (or a touchdown), plus how often we fail × our chance after they take over right here.

Punt

Our chance after they take over where our punts usually leave them. Punts from deep go farther: from our own 30 they typically start around their {{punt_from30}}, from our own 5 around their {{punt_from5}}, and almost never inside their {{punt_floor}}.

Field goal

How often we make it from this distance (the spot plus 17 yards) × our chance up 3 more with them receiving the kickoff, plus how often we miss × our chance after they take over at the spot of the kick (or their 20).

Chance of winning

The same estimate as our timeout work: the score, how many points the next drive is likely to produce, how much time is left, and how good each team is.

"Conversion % needed" is the break-even: if we convert at least that often, going is better than the best kick. Compare it with how often teams actually convert at that distance (the chart below).

The rates behind the card

How often teams convert and make kicks

These come from every FBS and FCS game since {{first_season}}. With "Brown's own" selected, each curve is shifted to match Brown's results over the same seasons: conversions {{brown_conv_note}}, field goals {{brown_fg_note}}, punts {{brown_punt_note}}.

How we did it

  • The data: play-by-play for every FBS and FCS game since {{first_season}} from PFF ({{games}} games).
  • Converting: {{conv_n}} third and fourth downs, fitted by yards to go, with a small penalty inside the 5 where the field is tight. Third and fourth downs convert at nearly the same rate at each distance, so we use both.
  • Field goals: {{fg_n}} tries, fitted by kick distance. A made kick puts us up 3 and they get the ball after the kickoff (we use their 25). A miss gives them the ball at the spot of the kick, or their 20.
  • Punts: we use where the other team actually starts after punts, which already includes returns, touchbacks and fair catches.
  • A successful conversion is treated as a first down right at the sticks (or a touchdown when it's 4th and goal), so the card slightly undersells going for it. Blocked kicks and punt-return touchdowns are counted only as far as they show up in the averages.
  • Brown's own rates move each whole curve up or down by one amount, fitted to Brown's results since 2023. That keeps a small sample from bending the curve in odd places. Brown's field goal numbers come from only {{brown_fg_n}} kicks, so treat that setting with care.
  • All numbers are changes in our chance of winning. Going from 30% to 32% is shown as +2%.
